# Lesson design

Use this guide to create a preparation card that can actually be rehearsed in the available time.

When grounding a card in published learning material, follow the source permissions in `../curriculum/sources.json`. English lessons may use `english_kouyu` sources only. A `reference_only` source can inform difficulty, naturalness, or activity design but cannot supply copied dialogue, instructions, or a directly adapted target list.

## Required inputs

- Topic: one of the topic bank entries or a learner-created situation.
- Duration: 5, 10, 20, 40, or a custom number of minutes.
- Level: beginner, intermediate, or advanced.
- Hint mode: `immersion`（先独立表达、仅在需要时帮忙）, `guided`（默认；目标处给中文意图提示）, or `learning`（更早给提示与关键词/句型）。The mode changes support timing and amount, not lesson difficulty or material volume.
- Optional personal objective: an upcoming trip, meeting, interview, or recurring difficulty.

## Scale by time

Build a visibly richer card as time increases. The three material types below are minimums, not a combined target count. A target tracked during role-play may be a word, phrase, or sentence pattern.

| Time | Key words | Phrases | Sentence patterns | Scene shape | Challenge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 5 min | 2–3 | 2 | 1 | one essential task | use 2 items |
| 10 min | 4–5 | 3–4 | 2 | one complete exchange | use 4 items |
| 20 min | 6–8 | 5–6 | 3–4 | main task plus one complication | use 6 items |
| 40 min | 8–12 | 7–10 | 5–7 | multi-stage scene with two complications | use 9 items and transfer 2 |

For a custom duration, interpolate conservatively. Fewer well-rehearsed targets are better than a crowded card.

## Scale by level

- Beginner: concrete high-frequency words, short reusable chunks, one-clause sentence frames, slow speech, and Chinese intent hints by default.
- Intermediate: common domain terms, natural collocations, connected sentences, and follow-up questions; use Chinese direction only at target elicitation points.
- Advanced: precise domain vocabulary, idiomatic collocations, authentic register, paraphrase, negotiation, and unexpected constraints; avoid translating unless requested.

Level changes difficulty and specificity, not card size. A 20-minute beginner card and a 20-minute advanced card should contain roughly the same number of items, while the advanced card uses more specialized language and complex sentence patterns.

## Topic bank

Use these as starting points, not fixed scripts:

- Grocery store: locating items, comparing options, dietary needs, promotions, checkout, substitutions.
- Gym: exercises and movement terms (for example squat, bench press, deadlift, reps, sets, range of motion), equipment, training plans, form cues, class booking, membership terms, and injury-safe alternatives. Do not reduce this topic to front-desk membership language.
- Café or restaurant: ordering, modifications, recommendations, problems, payment.
- Travel: airport, hotel, directions, transport, delays, lost items.
- Social: introductions, small talk, invitations, opinions, ending a conversation gracefully.
- Work: meetings, status updates, clarification, disagreement, interviews, negotiation.
- Daily services: clothes shopping, haircut, pharmacy or doctor visit, banking, delivery, and repairs.
- Travel details: airport check-in, immigration, public transport, asking directions, car rental, and disruptions.
- Interests: sports training, films, music, cooking, photography, games, and weekend plans.

When offering choices, show 10–12 varied candidates rather than only broad categories. Rotate or adapt candidates to the learner's goal and recent practice. Treat each candidate as a concrete scene (for example “健身房训练：动作与器械”) rather than a vague subject (“运动”).

Custom scenes must have a plausible counterpart, a concrete mission, and at least one decision or information gap.

## Preparation card

Present:

1. Scene, roles, and mission.
2. **Key words:** topic-specific nouns, verbs, or technical terms with concise Chinese meanings. Prefer concrete domain vocabulary over generic conversational words.
3. **Phrases/collocations:** reusable chunks with concise Chinese meanings.
4. **Sentence patterns:** complete, natural example sentences that demonstrate how the words and phrases work in context. Do not merely repeat a phrase with punctuation.
5. Two or three likely questions for 5–10 minutes; four to six for 20–40 minutes.
6. One likely difficulty or cultural/register note.
7. A measurable challenge based on time.

Keep the three material types visibly separated. Avoid filling a longer card by adding near-duplicates. For 20–40 minute cards, cover at least two sub-scenes or stages of the chosen topic.

Do not show a full dialogue. The preview should prepare the learner without making the role-play a memorization exercise.
